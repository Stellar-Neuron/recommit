When to Re-Commit: Temporal Abstraction Discovery for Long-Horizon Vision-Language Reasoning

Chen Li, Zhantao Yang, Fangyi Chen, Han Zhang, Anudeepsekhar Bolimera, Marios Savvides
Carnegie Mellon University
arXiv Paper Project Page

Adaptive commitment depth strictly Pareto-dominates the strongest fixed-depth baseline on both tasks, attaining up to 12.5 pp higher solve rate with ∼25% fewer primitive actions per episode.

Abstract

Long-horizon reasoning requires deciding not only what actions to take, but how deeply to commit before the next observation. This concept—determining how many primitive actions to execute open-loop between replans—defines a commitment depth that balances replanning costs against compounding execution errors. While most current long-horizon systems fix this depth as a hand-tuned scalar, we argue this is suboptimal. In this work, we treat commitment depth as a learnable, state-conditioned variable of the policy itself, instantiated as a model-native VLM that jointly predicts what to execute and for how long. On Sliding Puzzle and Sokoban, our adaptive policy Pareto-dominates every non-degenerate fixed-depth baseline, attaining up to 12.5 pp higher solve rate with ~25% fewer primitive actions per episode; despite a 7B backbone, it exceeds GPT-5.5 and Claude Sonnet on both tasks while every tested open-weight VLM scores 0% zero-shot. A theoretical model shows that, within the standard commitment-depth surrogate, state-conditioned depth strictly dominates fixed depth whenever the locally optimal depth varies across states.

Contributions

  • (C1) Problem formulation. We formalize when to re-commit as a budget-constrained optimization, identify the commitment depth surrogate within it, and show that the fixed-depth commitment adopted in existing practice is suboptimal whenever the locally optimal depth varies across states
  • (C2) Unified adaptive policy and infrastructure. We propose a single model-native VLM with a depth head and an action head sharing one backbone, trained jointly under one objective, along with a vision–language puzzle environment and a solver-grounded macro-action data pipeline
  • (C3) Pareto domination and mechanism. Adaptive strictly Pareto-dominates every non-degenerate fixed-depth baseline across budgets and tasks; a 7B model trained this way beats GPT-5.5 and Claude Sonnet while open-weight VLMs at the same scale score 0% zero-shot. The gain is mechanistically traceable: depth selection tracks state and task structure, and is stable across training budgets and seeds

Key Idea

  • Unified VLM policy with state-conditioned commitment depth.
  • Depth head πh emits h ∈ {1, 2, 4, 8}.
  • Action head πa generates a length-h sequence, executed open-loop.
  • Heads share backbone and one GRPO objective.
  • No solver or planner at evaluation.

Main Results

  • 56.3% solve rate on Sliding Puzzle.
  • 35.9% solve rate on Sokoban.
  • 24–25% fewer primitive actions than RL best fixed.
  • “Adaptive trajectories are straighter, not just shorter.”

Analysis

State-dependent commitment depth tracks progress-signal reliability. Mean depth decreases monotonically near→far, while failure rate increases farther from goal.

Task-conditioned commitment depth emerges during RL training. Sliding: h*=4 throughout. Sokoban: h* shifts from 4 early to 6 at middle/late checkpoints.

Adaptive trajectories are straighter, not just shorter. Adaptive matches or exceeds both fixed-depth baselines on every metric on both tasks.

The depth head stays at ∼0.91 log |H| of its uniform upper bound on both tasks, while the action head retains exploration capacity. Neither head collapses.
